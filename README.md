## What a Cashback Portal Actually Does

The mechanic is simple once you strip away the marketing language. Retailers run affiliate programs. They pay a commission to any third party that sends them a completed sale. Comparison sites, deal blogs, influencer links, and browser extensions all live on this commission. A cashback portal registers as one of those referrers, collects the commission, and hands most of it back to you as cash.

Three things follow from that:

1. **You pay the same price at checkout.** The cashback comes out of the merchant's marketing budget, not a markup on your order.
2. **The referral has to be tracked.** You have to click through from the portal to the store so the merchant can attribute the visit. No click-through, no commission, no cashback.
3. **Cashback clears after the merchant validates the order.** The platform can't pay you until the store confirms the sale went through and the return window closed.

Everything else — pending versus confirmed timing, category exclusions, why some rates are 1% and others are 10% — flows from those three rules.

## In-Store Cashback via Card-Linked Offers

Here's the part that surprises people: cashback isn't online-only. ShopBack US runs an in-store program built on card-linked offers, which means you link a US-issued card once, activate an offer in the app, and then just pay with that card at a participating shop. The cashback lands automatically — no coupons, no QR scans, no app open at the till.

The mechanic is different from online cashback. Online runs on affiliate cookies. In-store runs on the payment network. When you link a card (Chase, Citi, AMEX, Capital One, or Bank of America all work), the card-linked offer system registers your card with the merchant's offer program. When that card is used at a participating outlet, the payment network notifies the offer program, which passes the transaction to ShopBack. ShopBack credits the cashback in USD.

A few things worth knowing:

- **Card-linked cashback doesn't conflict with your card's own rewards.** Your Chase Ultimate Rewards or AMEX Membership Rewards are paid by the card issuer; in-store cashback is paid by the merchant. They run on independent rails, so both apply.
- **You have to activate the offer in the app before paying.** The system only watches for transactions on linked cards for offers you've explicitly opted into.
- **The price at the till is unchanged.** Cashback is added after the fact, from the merchant's offer budget.
- **Some chains include only specific outlets.** Check the merchant page in the app before visiting an unfamiliar location.

Across a year, in-store cashback compounds on the categories most people spend the most on without thinking — daily coffee, groceries, dining, the occasional pharmacy or salon visit. The setup is two minutes once; the earning is invisible after that.

## Stacking Cashback with Credit Cards and Coupons

The biggest mistake people make with cashback portals is treating them as a single decision instead of a stack. Cashback platforms and credit card rewards are funded by different parties and run on different rails, so they layer vertically. Running one without the other leaves money on the table.

The stack looks like this:

1. **Base layer: a rewards credit card.** Whatever card you already carry — a flat-rate card like Citi Double Cash or Capital One Quicksilver, or a category card like the Amazon Prime Visa Signature for Prime members — runs on every eligible purchase regardless of which cashback platform you use.
2. **Cashback layer: ShopBack (or Rakuten, or TopCashback — pick one per session).** Click through from the portal to the store, complete checkout in the same session, and the affiliate cashback lands on top of your card rewards.
3. **Optional rebate layer: Ibotta for grocery/CPG.** If your basket is heavy on household staples, brand-funded Ibotta rebates stack independently because they're verified by receipt scan, not affiliate click.

What does **not** stack: two cashback platforms in the same session. If you click through from Rakuten and then from ShopBack before checking out, the merchant attributes the sale to whichever platform sent the last click — and the other platform's tracking breaks entirely. Pick one portal per order.

A practical rule: **compare the base price first, then apply the cashback layer to whichever retailer wins on price.** If Walmart has the same product cheaper than Amazon, the price advantage usually outweighs the cashback delta. Cashback is a rebate on top of what you pay, not a discount at checkout.

## Common Tracking Mistakes and How to Avoid Them

Most "my cashback didn't track" complaints trace back to a handful of preventable mistakes. If you're going to use a cashback portal, get these right.

1. **Always start your session on ShopBack.** The click-through is what sets the tracking cookie. Going straight to the retailer breaks attribution. Install the browser extension as a backup for the moments you forget.
2. **Complete checkout in the same session, on the same device.** Don't switch from web to mobile mid-checkout. Don't close the tab and reopen the retailer directly.
3. **Empty your cart first for certain merchants.** Amazon and Walmart both require a clean cart — close the retailer's app, start fresh from ShopBack, and complete the purchase in one go. Items lingering in a cart from a previous non-ShopBack session can break tracking.
4. **Disable ad blockers and VPNs for the shopping session.** Ad blockers and VPNs can interfere with the tracking cookies that merchants rely on to attribute the sale.
5. **Use only coupon codes listed on ShopBack's merchant page.** External coupon codes from other sites can be excluded from cashback eligibility — the merchant's affiliate program may not pay commission on orders using unapproved codes.
6. **Don't click other affiliate or cashback links mid-session.** Facebook ads, Google ads, other loyalty extension links — any of these can overwrite the ShopBack tracking cookie and send the commission elsewhere.
7. **Accept all cookies from the store.** The merchant can only confirm a transaction was recorded if you accept the cookies that appear on their pages.

If a purchase doesn't show up in your ShopBack activity within 48 hours, file a missing-cashback claim through the app at **Account → Cashback → Missing Cashback**, with your order number, order date, and a screenshot of the order confirmation. There's a claim window stated on each merchant's page — don't wait past it.

## Who Should Use a Cashback Portal

Cashback portals aren't for everyone, and pretending otherwise is just marketing. Here's where they make sense and where they don't.

**They make sense if:**

- You shop online regularly — a few orders a month or more across retail, travel, food delivery, or marketplaces.
- You're willing to make starting on the portal a habit. Cashback attribution runs on click-through, so skipping the click on half your orders halves the return.
- You want cash, not gift-card credits. ShopBack, Rakuten, and TopCashback all pay in real dollars. Honey and Capital One Shopping rewards typically pay as gift cards.
- Your basket extends beyond a single retailer. The yearly return across a broad basket is where the math actually compounds.

**They don't make sense if:**

- You place a handful of online orders per year. The optimization work outweighs the return. A flat-rate general rewards card handles it with zero incremental effort.
- You can't be bothered to click through. Without the click, the platform earns nothing and pays you nothing.
- You're already committed to a single cashback platform and have a mature account there. Switching has switching costs, and two portals in the same session don't stack.

For most US shoppers who do meaningful online volume, the answer is: pick one platform, install its extension, make it the default starting point, and stack a rewards credit card underneath. ShopBack is a strong pick if your basket goes beyond Amazon into travel, marketplaces, fashion, groceries, or food delivery — its breadth across categories is where it earns its place. Rakuten is the mature default. TopCashback is the payout-option maximalist. Each wins in its scenario.
